Reading apps for iPhone Duo, compared honestly

There are now more than 100,000 apps optimised for iPhone Duo. Almost none of them were designed around the fold. A book is one of the few things that genuinely wants two facing pages, which is why the reader category is where the fold matters most — and why the choice is harder than it looks.

This page compares the three options most people end up weighing. It is written by the developer of one of them, so treat the framing with the scepticism it deserves — but the columns below are facts you can check yourself in the App Store, including the parts that do not flatter DuoFolio.

The short version

If you want a free reader and you already manage a calibre library, CalibReader is the obvious pick. It costs nothing, it has a real rating average, and it imports a whole calibre shelf in one go.

If you want the cover display to do something useful, and you care more about typography than library management, that is the case DuoFolio was built for. It is a one-time purchase with no library features at all.

If you only buy books from Apple, neither of these matters. Apple Books handles that fine.

What the fold actually changes

Most "iPhone Duo optimised" readers do one of two things with the fold. The first is to widen one column of text across the inner display — technically correct, visually unremarkable, and identical to reading on any large phone. The second is to paginate two columns side by side so the hinge falls in the gutter between them, which is what a paper book does when you hold it open.

The second approach is harder than it sounds, because a spread has to survive the phone changing shape. A book paginated for a full spread has to re-flow when you fold it half open, and the two halves have to stay in sync so you do not lose your place. That re-flow, done continuously, is most of the engineering in this category.

The idle problem nobody solves

The outer display is the part of a foldable that gets wasted. On a paperback, the cover is how you know what you are reading and how far in you are. On a folded Duo, the outer screen usually shows a clock. DuoFolio turns that screen into a cover card — art, title, progress — so the phone tells you what you are in the middle of without being opened at all.

Where a free reader wins

If you own a few hundred books and think of them as a library to be organised, a reader built around library management is the right tool, and DuoFolio is the wrong one. It has no shelves, no series grouping, no metadata editing, and no batch import. It opens one book at a time and tries to make that one book pleasant to read.

A note on paying for a reader

When a free app in the same category is competent, "why pay for it" is a fair question rather than a hostile one. The only honest answer is that the money does not buy more features — it buys the absence of them. No account, no telemetry, no library backend, no upgrade prompts, and no network code that could ever touch a file you imported. Whether that is worth the price depends entirely on how much you care that a reading app has nothing to report about you.
